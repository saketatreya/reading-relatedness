\begin{abstract}
What does agreement between representation geometry and a reference hierarchy establish?
We separate pairwise relatedness, agreement on individual four-source comparisons, and recovery
of reference splits in a reconstructed tree. Across frozen text, speech, protein, vision and
single-cell representations, these measurements support different conclusions. Local quartet
agreement ranges from \Result{summary.quartet_min} to \Result{summary.quartet_max}, but neighbor joining
and FastME recover only a minority of the specified splits in the four larger populations.
Nevertheless, their reconstructed trees retain substantial quartet agreement: low exact split
recall does not imply absence of hierarchical information. Matched readout comparisons expose a
second distinction. For 36 languages, simply centering raw centroids changes the cosine association
with genealogy, adjusted for typology, from
\Result{readouts.language.36.layers.12.raw.cosine.r} to
\Result{readouts.language.36.layers.12.raw_centered.cosine.r}, approximately matching the aligned
readout. Matching the origin also largely removes an apparent alignment-population effect.
Exact additive-distance controls validate both reconstruction pipelines; a separate Brownian
feature simulation illustrates how perturbations affect graded agreement and split recovery.
The resulting protocol treats the origin, normalization, layer, aggregation and reconstruction
method as parts of the measurement. It supports specific claims about accessible structure,
without equating a failed readout with information absent from the representation.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
representation geometry, hierarchical structure, phylogenetic reconstruction, readout sensitivity,

\section{Introduction}

Related sources often occupy nearby positions in a learned representation. This observation can
support several progressively more specific interpretations: the representation distinguishes
related from unrelated sources; it agrees with local branching relationships; or it permits
reconstruction of a particular hierarchy. The measurements needed for these interpretations are
different. A high nearest-neighbor score does not determine a tree, and a low rate of exact split
recovery does not establish that a reconstructed tree is uninformative.

Consider the vision case in this study. For DINOv2 centroids of CIFAR-100 classes, neighbor joining
recovers only \Result{domains.vision.metrics.cosine.nj.recovered} of
\Result{domains.vision.n_reference_splits} specified semantic splits using cosine dissimilarity.
Yet the resulting tree agrees with
\Result{domains.vision.metrics.cosine.nj.tree_quartet} of the reference-resolved quartets.
These are compatible descriptions of the same reconstruction. An exact split requires every
source to fall on its correct side; quartet agreement measures how much local structure remains.
The reference itself is a semantic taxonomy, so neither result is evidence about evolutionary
descent.

The geometry being evaluated also requires specification. Changing the origin leaves Euclidean
distances unchanged but can substantially change cosine dissimilarities. Source-specific
alignment, radial normalization and layer selection introduce further choices. Consequently, a
change in an association after preprocessing need not isolate the operation to which it is
attributed. We use matched comparisons to determine what these choices change in the extracted
representations, rather than select a readout by its agreement with the target.

Our contribution is an empirical methodology with three components. First, five case studies
compare local relatedness and hierarchical recovery with a shared evaluator and two distance-based
tree estimators. Second, matched language readouts and a speech layer sweep identify consequential
measurement choices, including an origin effect that explains much of an apparent alignment effect.
Third, exact-distance controls and a separate feature-space simulation distinguish estimator
validation from an illustrative perturbation mechanism. Together they support a more precise
question than whether a representation ``contains a phylogeny'': \emph{which reference relationships
are accessible through a declared readout, at which resolution?}

This question connects representation probing to established work on distance-based reconstruction.
Language vectors and multilingual encoders have been used to study genealogical and typological
structure \citep{ostling-tiedemann-2017-continuous,bjerva-etal-2019-language,rama-etal-2020-probing}.
The distinction between a representation's information and a probe's accessibility is central to
probe interpretation \citep{hewitt-liang-2019-designing,pimentel-etal-2020-information}; structural
probes explicitly learn a geometry for a target relation \citep{hewitt-manning-2019-structural}.
Here we study specified distance readouts without training a supervised tree probe. Our comparisons
also complement evidence that embedding geometry depends on influential dimensions and
normalization \citep{timkey-van-schijndel-2021-bark}.

\section{A protocol for measuring accessible structure}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Sources, representations and references.}
A source is a language, species, image class or cell type. Each source is reduced to a vector;
Table~\ref{tab:populations} states the aggregation and the meaning of its reference hierarchy.
The five populations are case studies with different sample sizes and reference resolutions.
They are not exchangeable replications of one evolutionary process; text and speech also share
language histories. We fix one primary readout per domain and assess alternatives in explicit
sensitivity analyses. Extraction details and reference definitions are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:extraction}; code, data and the complete result record are available at
\url{https://github.com/saketatreya/reading-relatedness}.

Only the text experiment uses Procrustes alignment. Its rotations and per-language scales are fitted
on 812 parallel sentences; centroids use the remaining 200. Its default origin is the grand mean
over both portions, so the origin is transductive. Speech, vision and cell vectors are pooled
in the encoder's coordinates. Protein vectors concatenate coordinate-standardized gene embeddings,
with missing gene blocks set to zero. These reductions discard within-source variation; all claims
below concern the resulting vectors, not every statistic of the original representation clouds.

\paragraph{Three resolutions of agreement.}
For source vectors $x_i$, we evaluate Euclidean distance and cosine dissimilarity
$D^C_{ij}=1-x_i^\top x_j/(\|x_i\|\|x_j\|)$. We call the latter a dissimilarity because it need not
satisfy the triangle inequality. The reference matrix $G$ is the path distance in the coded
hierarchy with unit edges; it is not a matrix of divergence dates. We report:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Pairwise relatedness:} Pearson correlation $r$ between the upper triangles of $D$
and $G$, and nearest-neighbor group purity. Purity is averaged over tied nearest neighbors and
compared with $\sum_g n_g(n_g-1)/[n(n-1)]$ for the stated grouping.
\item \textbf{Local quartet agreement $Q_D$:} for each reference-resolved set $\{i,j,k,l\}$,
compare the minimizing pairing among
$D_{ij}+D_{kl}$, $D_{ik}+D_{jl}$ and $D_{il}+D_{jk}$ with the reference pairing.
This is a local topological statistic, not a purely pairwise metric statistic.
\item \textbf{Reconstructed hierarchy:} infer an unrooted tree, then report both reference-split
recall $S=|\mathcal S(\hat T)\cap\mathcal S(G)|/|\mathcal S(G)|$ and quartet agreement $Q_{\hat T}$
of that tree. A split and its complement count once; both sides must contain at least two sources.
Tree quartets use topology with unit edges, independently of estimated branch lengths.
\end{enumerate}

The empirical references contain unresolved relationships. We therefore score only quartets that
the reference resolves and do not label split recall ``exact tree recovery.'' In particular, we
do not penalize an inferred tree merely for resolving a reference polytomy. Full-tree exactness and
normalized Robinson--Foulds distance are additionally recorded for the fully resolved synthetic
trees. Predicted quartet ties receive fractional credit; $1/3$ is the uniform-guess baseline.

\paragraph{Estimators and uncertainty.}
We compare neighbor joining (NJ; \citealt{saitou1987neighbor}) with FastME 2.1.6.3 balanced minimum
evolution, using balanced taxon addition and its NNI and SPR searches
\citep{lefort2015fastme}. FastME broadens the reconstruction comparison, but both procedures act on
the same distance matrices and share a minimum-evolution motivation. Their agreement cannot
establish a limit for other estimators or representation statistics.

We enumerate all quartets for populations of at most 30 sources and otherwise sample 40,000
distinct quartets uniformly, sharing the sample across readouts and methods. Unadjusted $r$,
$Q_D$ and purity use 2,000 source-label permutations, with $p=(h+1)/2001$, where $h$ counts
permutations at least as extreme as the observed value; $r$ is two-sided and agreement statistics
upper-tailed. These test a random-label null, not a causal account of
relatedness. Pairs and quartets sharing sources are not independent replicates. Adjusted language
correlations are descriptive residual correlations, accompanied by leave-one-source-out sensitivity
ranges rather than conditional-Mantel significance claims \citep{guillot2013mantel}. Because the
analysis uses precomputed source centroids, an observation-level bootstrap is not available. The
analysis is retrospective: it reuses previously extracted representations, and its protocol and
controls were fixed before the FastME comparisons were run.

\section{Local agreement and exact splits give different readings}
\label{sec:crossmodal}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig1_recovery.pdf}
\caption{Three resolutions of agreement, evaluated on the same sources and reference hierarchies.
Color distinguishes Euclidean and cosine readouts; filled circles and open squares distinguish NJ
and FastME in (b,c). The dotted line in (a,b) is the uniform quartet-guess baseline, not a confidence
threshold. Split recall in (c) has no shared chance baseline across these differently resolved
references. Complete counts and random-label tests appear in Appendix~\ref{app:fullresults}.}
\label{fig:recovery}
\end{figure}

Local quartet agreement exceeds $1/3$ in all ten domain/readout combinations, ranging from
\Result{summary.quartet_min} to \Result{summary.quartet_max} (Figure~\ref{fig:recovery}a).
Nearest-neighbor purity also exceeds its group-size baseline throughout. This does not imply
uniformly strong distance-matrix correlation: speech has $r=\Result{domains.speech.metrics.euclid.distance_r}$
under Euclidean distance, with random-label $p=\Result{domains.speech.metrics.euclid.permutation.distance_r_p}$;
the Euclidean protein correlation is also weak. Pairwise distances are therefore not uniformly
faithful to the reference, even before reconstruction is considered.

Exact reference-split recall is low for both reconstruction methods in the four larger populations,
but it is method-dependent. For text, NJ recovers
\Result{domains.text.metrics.euclid.nj.recovered}/\Result{domains.text.n_reference_splits} splits with
Euclidean distance and \Result{domains.text.metrics.cosine.nj.recovered}/\Result{domains.text.n_reference_splits}
with cosine; FastME recovers \Result{domains.text.metrics.euclid.fastme.recovered}/\Result{domains.text.n_reference_splits}
under each readout. Protein changes from no recovered splits with NJ to
\Result{domains.protein.metrics.cosine.fastme.recovered}/\Result{domains.protein.n_reference_splits}
with cosine FastME. Speech recovers one of seven and vision two of 25 under both methods and metrics.
The cell case recovers one of only two specified splits. It provides a small illustration, not a
precise estimate of recovery on developmental trees.

The reconstructed trees nevertheless preserve graded hierarchical agreement
(Figure~\ref{fig:recovery}b). Vision reaches
$Q_{NJ}=\Result{domains.vision.metrics.cosine.nj.tree_quartet}$ under cosine even though its split
recall is \Result{domains.vision.metrics.cosine.nj.split_recall}.
For protein, cosine FastME reaches $Q_{FM}=\Result{domains.protein.metrics.cosine.fastme.tree_quartet}$,
compared with $Q_{NJ}=\Result{domains.protein.metrics.cosine.nj.tree_quartet}$.
Conversely, FastME reduces vision quartet agreement while retaining the same split count.
Optimizing the reconstruction objective need not improve every reference-based score. These
results support limited exact split recovery under the evaluated pipelines, together with
substantial partial recovery; they do not support absence of hierarchical information.

Reference choice also affects apparently simple comparisons. Vision's cosine nearest-neighbor
purity is \Result{domains.vision.metrics.cosine.nn_purity} for the five authored metaclasses, but
\Result{domains.vision.metrics.cosine.nn_finer} for the 20 official CIFAR superclasses, whose chance
level is \Result{domains.vision.metrics.cosine.nn_finer_chance}. Protein uses a coarse taxonomic
grouping and uneven gene coverage, rather than a dated molecular phylogeny. These distinctions
limit biological interpretation and make a pooled cross-domain recovery rate inappropriate.

\section{Readout choices can explain apparent structural effects}
\label{sec:readouts}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig2_readouts.pdf}
\caption{Matched readout comparisons. (a) The same 36 languages and layer: changing the origin of
raw centroids substantially changes the adjusted cosine association. Bars span source-deletion
estimates; they are sensitivity ranges, not confidence intervals. (b) Evaluating the same 36
languages after fitting on 36 or 89: subtracting each set's evaluation-centroid mean largely closes
the gap. (c) Speech quartet agreement and participation ratio vary across layers. The dashed
PR curve uses the right axis; the horizontal dotted line marks quartet chance.}
\label{fig:readouts}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The origin precedes the radial quotient.}
On a 36-language subset, let $G_U$ be URIEL family-vector distance and $T$ URIEL imputed syntactic
distance \citep{littell-etal-2017-uriel}. Unlike the coded hierarchy in Section~\ref{sec:crossmodal},
these are feature-vector distances. We compare $\mathrm{corr}(D,G_U\mid T)$ using a fixed pair set.
At the primary text layer, L12, raw Euclidean and cosine readouts yield
\Result{readouts.language.36.layers.12.raw.euclid.r} and
\Result{readouts.language.36.layers.12.raw.cosine.r}, respectively. Subtracting the mean of these
36 raw centroids leaves Euclidean distances unchanged, but raises the cosine association to
\Result{readouts.language.36.layers.12.raw_centered.cosine.r}.
Procrustes alignment fitted on these same languages yields
\Result{readouts.language.36.layers.12.aligned36.cosine.r}. Thus the large adjusted association
does not require the source-specific alignment in this comparison. All four extracted layers and
both evaluation populations are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:readouts}; L12 is the primary text
layer throughout, not one chosen to maximize this association.

For an explicit origin $o$, cosine compares the directions of $x_i-o$ and $x_j-o$. It removes
positive rescaling of these vectors, but it is not translation-invariant. Euclidean distance obeys
\[
 \|x_i-x_j\|^2=\|x_i-o\|^2+\|x_j-o\|^2
 -2\|x_i-o\|\|x_j-o\|\cos\theta_{ij}(o),
\]
so its radial and angular decomposition itself depends on $o$. In the 89-fitted frame restricted
to the 36 languages, L12 norm correlates with tokenizer fragmentation (subwords per character) at
\Result{readouts.language.36.layers.12.aligned89.norm_fragmentation_r}.
This association motivates checking radial sensitivity. It does not prove that the radial
component contains only nuisance or that removing it recovers a uniquely correct geometry.
Norms can carry useful information in other settings \citep{oyama-etal-2023-norm}.

\paragraph{Matching the origin changes the alignment-population interpretation.}
For those same 36 languages, cosine partial correlation is
\Result{readouts.language.36.layers.12.aligned36.cosine.r} when alignment is fitted on 36 and
\Result{readouts.language.36.layers.12.aligned89.cosine.r} when their vectors are taken from the
89-language fit. This comparison changes both the fitted transformations and the origin.
After subtracting the mean of the evaluated 36 centroids within each frame, the values become
\Result{readouts.language.36.layers.12.aligned36_centered.cosine.r} and
\Result{readouts.language.36.layers.12.aligned89_centered.cosine.r}.
The original gap therefore cannot be attributed to the alignment population independently of
centering. This matched-origin operation is label-free but transductive: it uses the evaluation
population to define its origin, and is not an independently validated rule for future sources.

\paragraph{Effective dimension diagnoses a readout, not its information capacity.}
For centered source vectors with singular values $s_j$, participation ratio is
$\mathrm{PR}=(\sum_j s_j^2)^2/\sum_j s_j^4$.
The final speech layer has PR \Result{readouts.speech.24.participation_ratio}, out of a maximum
centered rank of 29, and mean pairwise cosine
\Result{readouts.speech.24.mean_pairwise_cosine}. Its cosine quartet agreement is
\Result{readouts.speech.24.metrics.cosine.distance_quartet}, compared with
\Result{readouts.speech.12.metrics.cosine.distance_quartet} at hidden-state index 12, whose PR is
\Result{readouts.speech.12.participation_ratio}. PR flags concentration of the measured variation;
it neither proves that no hierarchy is encoded nor identifies the best layer without evaluation.
Layer sweeps should accompany such diagnostics.

\paragraph{Matching pairs changes the control comparison.}
A similar attribution issue arises in the language typology control. Within six families, an
eight-configuration aligned Euclidean ensemble yields an adjusted genealogical correlation of
\Result{language_appendix.descent.imputed_all.r} with imputed typology over 94 pairs, versus
\Result{language_appendix.descent.observed_matched.r} with co-observed typological features over
81 pairs. On the \emph{same} 81 pairs, the imputed-control result is
\Result{language_appendix.descent.imputed_matched.r}.
The unmatched contrast therefore provides no evidence for a large imputation penalty. The retained
descriptive association is useful, but its interpretation requires specifying both feature
provenance and pair availability (Appendix~\ref{app:language}).

\section{Validation and a controlled perturbation example}
\label{sec:synthetic}

\paragraph{An exact-distance control.}
We first give the estimators the exact path-distance matrices of balanced binary trees with 8 and
256 leaves. Both NJ and FastME recover every unique reference split and every tested quartet.
These controls verify the distance-to-tree and scoring pipelines under exact additivity. They do
not establish that either estimator is optimal on the empirical dissimilarities.

\paragraph{A separate feature-space simulation.}
We then generate 64-dimensional leaf vectors by summing independent $\mathcal N(0,I_{64})$ edge
increments on a balanced depth-8 tree. If $d_T$ is its unit-edge distance, then
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\|Z_i-Z_j\|^2/64\right]=d_T(i,j).
\]
The observed finite-dimensional Euclidean distances are \emph{not} exact additive tree distances;
neither are their cosine counterparts. This is a noisy positive example distinct from the oracle
control. We evaluate all 253 unique internal splits with the same scorer used above, and separately
report the five unique splits induced by the two deepest levels.

We apply two feature-space perturbations: a positive radial scale $e^{k_N r_i}$, with fixed
$r_i\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$, and donor mixing
$Z_i\mapsto(1-k_C)Z_i+k_CZ_{d(i)}$, with a fixed donor from the opposite root subtree. We vary
one amplitude at a time, sharing the tree, scales, donors and quartet sample across doses within
each of five seeds. This interpolation is a sufficient construction for studying altered geometry;
it is not a model fitted to contact, horizontal transfer or encoder training.

Without perturbation, mean Euclidean NJ split recall is
\Result{synthetic_mean.clean.euclid.nj.split_recall}, while FastME yields
\Result{synthetic_mean.clean.euclid.fastme.split_recall}.
Positive radial scaling reduces Euclidean correlation but leaves the cosine measurements
unchanged, as required algebraically. At mixing weight $k_C=0.35$, Euclidean distance correlation
is \Result{synthetic_mean.mixing_0.35.euclid.distance_r}, local quartet agreement is
\Result{synthetic_mean.mixing_0.35.euclid.distance_quartet}, and all-split recall is
\Result{synthetic_mean.mixing_0.35.euclid.nj.split_recall} for NJ and
\Result{synthetic_mean.mixing_0.35.euclid.fastme.split_recall} for FastME.
At this dose neither method recovers any of the five deepest splits, even while many of the
remaining splits survive. Whether topology appears to degrade before distances therefore depends
on which splits are scored. Reporting the full hierarchy avoids reducing the response to a selected
success/failure boundary.

\paragraph{What the recovery guarantee explains.}
For an additive binary-tree distance $d_T$ with minimum edge length $b>0$, Atteson's sufficient
condition for NJ recovery is $\|\hat d-d_T\|_\infty<b/2$ \citep{atteson1999performance}.
Exceeding this radius removes that guarantee; it does not require NJ to fail. NJ can succeed
outside it \citep{mihaescu2009neighbor}. The condition concerns worst-case distance error relative
to edge length, whereas a global correlation measures average variation across pairs.

Under the additional idealized model $\hat d=d+\varepsilon\eta$, treating upper-triangle entries
as the variables and assuming $\operatorname{Cov}(d,\eta)=0$, nonzero $\operatorname{Var}(d)$ and
finite second moments,
\[
 \operatorname{corr}(d,\hat d)=
 \left(1+\varepsilon^2\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\eta)}{\operatorname{Var}(d)}\right)^{-1/2}
 =1-\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\eta)}{\operatorname{Var}(d)}+O(\varepsilon^4).
\]
This separates a global correlation scale from an exact-recovery guarantee. The empirical
embeddings and the donor-mixing experiment are not assumed to satisfy this noise model. Thus the
theorem, the illustrative simulation and the empirical observations provide different kinds of
evidence; none identifies the cause of reconstruction errors in the real populations.

\section{Discussion and limits}
\label{sec:discussion}

The strongest supported conclusion is that accessible structure has both a \emph{resolution} and
a \emph{readout}. Local agreement can coexist with low exact split recall, while reconstructed
trees still preserve many reference quartets. Meanwhile, apparently substantive differences between
readouts can arise from unmatched origins or pair sets. A useful reporting protocol therefore
declares the source aggregation, origin, normalization, layer and fitted population; evaluates
relatedness, local quartets and reconstructed-tree agreement separately; validates the scorer on
known trees; and compares proposed explanations under matched conditions.

These checks do not select a universally correct geometry. A common orthogonal transformation
preserves norms, angles and Euclidean distances alike, so orthogonal invariance does not uniquely
privilege cosine. Source-specific rotations, scaling and centering are different operations.
Identifiability results for specified contrastive objectives and generative models
\citep{zimmermann2021contrastive} do not by themselves justify an invariant readout across the
heterogeneous pretrained encoders studied here. Label-free diagnostics such as PR, radial
associations and origin sensitivity can reveal fragile measurements; target agreement is still
needed to assess what a readout recovers.

The experiments also have substantive limits. The empirical references are coarse and partly
authored. Protein retrieval uses gene-name queries rather than verified orthology, with strongly
uneven coverage and zero-filled missing blocks. The cell population contains only eight types and
two scored groups. Text uses parallel translated sentences, so corpus and translation effects may
contribute to the measured associations. Cloud aggregation excludes covariance, multimodality and
subspace-specific structure. Two language cloud statistics are reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:clouds}; they do not rule out richer distributional readouts.

Likewise, neither a small \emph{mean} four-point residual nor its diameter normalization rules out
local distortion at short branches. Our results do not rule out hyperbolic readouts or alternative
tree embeddings, and they do not identify a training-objective mechanism, which would require a
controlled training intervention. Nonlinear distance transformations and learned probes remain possible
alternatives. The present results justify precise statements about the tested measurements:
partial hierarchical structure is accessible, exact reference splits are recovered unevenly,
and the preprocessing that defines the geometry materially changes the answer.

\section{Extraction, hierarchy definitions and reproducibility}
\label{app:extraction}

The analysis reads fixed representation caches and writes a single JSON result record with input
SHA256 hashes, software versions, FastME options, scores and inferred empirical splits. Every
result value, table and figure in this paper is generated from that record. Tests cover
known topologies, complementary splits, rerooting, row permutations, unresolved quartets, ties,
random-label nulls, radial invariance, translation invariance of Euclidean distance, Newick parsing,
and exact additive recovery by both estimators. All encoders are used frozen. The extraction
scripts record the model identifiers used; because upstream checkpoints can change, the saved
caches, rather than bitwise re-extraction, define the analysis.

\paragraph{Text.}
XLM-R-large \citep{conneau-etal-2020-unsupervised} embeds 1,012 FLORES-200 devtest sentences per
language \citep{nllb2022} in float32, with a maximum token length of 256. Mean pooling excludes
special tokens. The primary readout, labeled L12, is the output of transformer block 12 with
zero-based indexing (\texttt{hidden\_states[13]}). Alternative readouts are L4, L8 and L18,
plus pooling over the 80 most frequent nonspecial subwords, with mean-pool fallback for empty
matches. Sentence frequencies use the full corpus. The language hierarchy consists of coded
macrofamily, branch, genus and language paths; its pair distances are prefix-tree distances.
URIEL family vectors are a separate reference used for the adjusted language analyses.

For language $\ell$, the alignment divides representations by the RMS deviation of its training
cloud, estimates an orthogonal matrix by five generalized Procrustes iterations using centered
training clouds, then applies that matrix to the \emph{uncentered}, scaled observations. Each
language's centroid is its test mean minus the grand mean over all aligned observations in the
fitted population. Thus rotation fitting uses paired training sentences, while the origin includes test
observations. The 36-language and 89-language alignments are separately fitted. Our additional
centering subtracts the mean of the vectors in the evaluation population; it does not refit
rotations. The source-deletion analysis holds these fitted alignments fixed, recomputes an evaluation
mean when that readout calls for one, and refits the residual regression.

\paragraph{Speech.}
XLS-R-300m \citep{babu2022xlsr} embeds 100 utterances per language from FLEURS
\citep{conneau2022fleurs}. Time pooling excludes padding, then utterance vectors are averaged.
Layers use \texttt{hidden\_states[6,12,18,24]}, a convention distinct from the text labels. The
primary speech row uses index 12; the final layer is evaluated explicitly rather than substituted
for it. Reference paths group Romance, Germanic, Slavic and Indo-Aryan under Indo-European and
retain the other coded families separately. Nearest-neighbor purity uses the family level.

\paragraph{Protein.}
The checkpoint is \texttt{facebook/esm2\_t33\_650M\_UR50D} \citep{lin2023evolutionary}.
Fifteen gene-name queries per vertebrate are sent to UniProt, preferring reviewed entries and
otherwise taking the first returned sequence. Sequences are capped at 1,022 residues. Pooled
embeddings for each gene are standardized coordinate-wise across species with available sequences;
the 15 blocks are concatenated into a 19,200-dimensional species vector. Missing blocks are zero.
This retrieval does not verify one-to-one orthology. Missingness ranges from no blocks to 14 of 15,
and can itself affect similarity. All species-specific counts and gene names are saved in the
result record. The reference uses class/subgroup/species paths (e.g., Mammal/Euarch/human), not
branch-length estimates or divergence dates. Its six nontrivial splits are a limited taxonomic
target.

\paragraph{Vision.}
DINOv2-base \citep{oquab2024dinov2} supplies the final CLS representation after resizing images to
$224\times224$. All 100 test images per CIFAR-100 class are averaged
\citep{krizhevsky2009learning}. The 20 official superclasses are grouped into five authored
metaclasses: animal, plant, house, outdoor and vehicle. The exact mapping and all leaf paths are
saved in the result record. The reference therefore has 25 scored semantic splits. We distinguish purity at
these two grouping levels throughout.

\paragraph{Single-cell.}
Geneformer V2-104M is used with its transcriptome tokenizer and cell embedding extraction at
\texttt{emb\_layer=-1}; \citet{theodoris2023transfer} describes the Geneformer framework rather
than this exact checkpoint version. The processed PBMC3k labels \citep{zheng2017massively} define
eight cell-type centroids: four lymphoid types (CD4 T, CD8 T, B and NK), three myeloid types (CD14
monocytes, FCGR3A monocytes and dendritic cells), and megakaryocytes. The latter singleton supplies
no nontrivial split. These are cell-type groupings, not lineage-tracing observations.

\paragraph{Within-family association.}
